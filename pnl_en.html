Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP)

Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) examines connections among neurological processes, language and learned behavioral patterns. Its methods are commonly presented in communication and personal-development contexts.

Origin: When and Where Was It Created?

NLP emerged in the 1970s in the United States, particularly around the University of California, Santa Cruz.

Richard Bandler and John Grinder developed early NLP models by studying communication patterns associated with several prominent therapists of the period.

Who Can Work with NLP?

NLP training is usually offered through free-standing certification courses. It is not equivalent to a regulated health profession, and its use in clinical contexts must respect professional boundaries.

  • People certified by NLP training institutes.
  • Qualified professionals who use selected communication tools within their own regulated scope.

Uses and Care

NLP is often applied to communication, goal-setting and personal-development exercises, but its claims should be assessed critically.

Common uses:

  • Personal development and goal-setting.
  • Communication and leadership training.
  • Habit and behavior reflection.

Important limitations:

  • NLP should not replace psychiatric or evidence-based psychological treatment for serious conditions.
  • Work involving severe trauma should be conducted by appropriately qualified mental-health professionals.
