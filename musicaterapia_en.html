Music Therapy

Music Therapy is an integrative practice that uses music, rhythm, melody and sound in a structured process to support physical and mental health goals. It is conducted by qualified professionals.

Origin: When and Where Did It Develop?

Although music and healing have been connected for centuries, modern music therapy became organized as a profession during the 20th century, especially in the United States after World War II.

Volunteer musicians worked in military hospitals, helping increase interest in specialized training. In Brazil, dedicated university programs began appearing in the 1970s.

Who Can Work as a Music Therapist?

In Brazil, professional practice requires specialized academic training under current legislation.

  • Professionals with an undergraduate degree in Music Therapy.
  • Professionals who meet the legally recognized postgraduate training requirements.

Uses and Care

Music therapy may be used in different clinical and educational contexts, always according to an individual assessment.

Common areas of use:

  • Autism spectrum: communication and sensory-regulation support.
  • Neurodegenerative conditions: cognitive and emotional support.
  • Motor rehabilitation: support for movement and speech recovery.
  • Mental health: complementary support for stress, anxiety and mood-related difficulties.

Situations requiring extra care:

  • Specific sound-triggered neurological conditions.
  • Severe sound sensitivity.
  • Acute psychiatric crises requiring medical stabilization.
